\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The Modernization Gain Hides a Regression}
\label{sec:headline}

\cref{tab:fullpipeline} reports per-video HOTA for the 2017 baseline and the modernized tracker.
With the detector confidence gate raised to $0.45$, the modern tracker beats the baseline on every
one of the six videos, lifting mean HOTA from \baselineHOTA{} to \modernHOTA{} ($+\deltaHOTA$) at a
real-time \fpsRealtime{}~FPS (per-clip tuning reaches \modernBestHOTA{}). This is the expected,
unsurprising result. What the mean hides is the middle row: at its \emph{default} gate (confidence
$0.25$, no secondary NMS), the modernized tracker scores \modernDefaultHOTA{} -- still
$+\deltaDefaultHOTA$ above baseline on average, but it \textbf{regresses below the 2017 baseline on
TUD-Campus} ($35.03$ vs.\ $39.86$). A decade-newer detector and appearance model make the tracker
worse on that clip. The rest of this section explains why, and shows that the fix is a gate rather
than a better model.

\subsection{The Gain Is Detection-Dominated}
\label{sec:decomp}

Decomposing the gain on the combined MOT16 split (\cref{tab:decomp}) shows it is detection-dominated:
the detection term rises by $\detaGain$ HOTA points while the association term rises by only
$\assaGain$. MOTA barely moves ($\baselineMOTA \rightarrow \modernMOTA$), which is why we report HOTA.
The same conclusion holds from the association side: replacing the detector with a ground-truth-box
oracle isolates association, and a dedicated OSNet then reaches \osnetGT{} HOTA while a generic,
non-re-ID-trained ImageNet backbone reaches \timmGT{} -- a gap of under one HOTA point
(OSNet-AIN \osnetainGT{} is intermediate). Once detection is held perfect, association quality is
nearly detector-bound and the appearance model is almost free; the modernization gain lives in the
detector.

This makes the \emph{quality} of the detection stream, not its raw quantity, the operative variable.
\cref{tab:detector} reports YOLOv8m precision, recall, and F1 against ground truth at
IoU~$\geq 0.5$. Recall is uniformly high (mean $0.831$), but precision is strongly clip-dependent:
high on clean sequences (TUD-Stadtmitte $0.907$, PETS09 $0.858$) and low on the dense, low-resolution
clips (TUD-Campus $0.505$, KITTI-17 $0.514$), where the detector emits roughly as many false positives
as true ones. This per-clip precision is the quantity that predicts how much each clip is hurt by
ungated detections.

\subsection{Lowering the Gate Lowers HOTA}
\label{sec:hurt}

We sweep the detector confidence threshold per video over $\{0.15, 0.25, 0.35, 0.45\}$, holding
everything else fixed; lowering the threshold raises recall and lowers precision.
\cref{fig:hota_vs_conf} shows that raising the gate \textbf{never lowers HOTA on any of the
\nVideos{} videos} ($\nHurt/\nVideos$): admitting more low-confidence detections only ever hurts. What
varies is the magnitude. On the low-precision clips the curve climbs steeply (TUD-Campus
$32.5\rightarrow46.3$, KITTI-17 $42.3\rightarrow51.5$ from conf $0.15$ to $0.45$), while on the
high-precision clips it is nearly flat and saturates early (PETS09 peaks at conf $0.25$ and is within
half a point thereafter).

This magnitude is predicted by per-clip detector precision. \cref{fig:money} plots, for each video,
the HOTA gained by raising the gate from $0.15$ to $0.45$ against the clip's detector precision
(\cref{tab:detector}); the two are strongly anti-correlated (Pearson $r = \corrPearson$): the less
precise the detector on a clip, the more that clip is hurt by ungated detections and the more it gains
from a higher gate. This is the paper's central, quantitative form of ``more detections hurt.''

A second, coarser recall lever behaves less uniformly and is instructive about the mechanism. On the
two dense clips we sweep the detector input resolution (\cref{fig:imgsz}). On TUD-Campus, raising the
input size drives HOTA monotonically \emph{down}
($\imgszLow \rightarrow \imgszMid \rightarrow \imgszHigh$), but on KITTI-17 it rises
($44.8 \rightarrow 47.3$). Unlike the confidence threshold, which is a pure admission gate on a fixed
detection set, resolution changes \emph{which} detections exist: the extra boxes recovered at higher
resolution are mostly false positives on TUD-Campus but mostly true small instances on KITTI-17. The
effect is therefore not ``more boxes are always bad'' but the sharper claim the confidence sweep
isolates: admitting \emph{low-confidence} detections is what hurts, in proportion to how imprecise the
detector is on that clip.

\subsection{The Dropped Gate: a Restoration Ablation}
\label{sec:ablation}

To attribute the default regression to the dropped gating contract rather than to the detector, we
restore the original DeepSORT gate -- $\text{min\_confidence}=0.3$ with NMS at overlap~$0.7$ -- on top
of the otherwise unchanged modern pipeline (\cref{fig:ablation}). Restoring the gate recovers HOTA on
the dense clips (mean $\ablationRecover$ over TUD-Campus and KITTI-17 relative to the ungated default)
while leaving the clean clips within roughly a point. Simply raising the confidence threshold to
$0.45$ recovers even more on TUD-Campus ($46.3$ vs.\ the restored gate's $39.2$), consistent with the
gate, not the detector, being the cause. Because the restored filter is the same one the original
method applied, re-enabled by configuration alone, the recovered HOTA is causally attributable to it.

\subsection{A Per-Clip Precision-Gating Guideline}
\label{sec:tuning}

The practical consequence is a simple per-clip policy: set the detector confidence threshold from the
clip's precision regime rather than trusting a global default. On the worst sequence, the ungated
default loses to the 2017 baseline by $\tudUntuned$ HOTA; raising the TUD-Campus threshold to $0.45$
moves it to $+\tudGain$ over baseline, and the change is confined to that sequence's preset, leaving
the other five untouched. This single per-video override is what turns the average-but-uneven default
of \cref{tab:fullpipeline} into a win on all six videos, at a mean real-time throughput of
\fpsRealtime{}~FPS.
